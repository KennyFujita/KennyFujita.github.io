About

I am a robotics engineer. Before that I spent six years on control theory — multi-agent systems, hierarchical optimal control, and communication delay compensation — as a doctoral student at Nagoya University, a researcher at MAZIN Inc., and a postdoctoral researcher at Tokyo City University.

This site keeps both halves in one place: the papers I wrote while I was a researcher, and whatever I am building, reading and travelling to now.

Research

How optimally can information be aggregated?

Large organisations such as companies and universities are built as hierarchies. Each group compresses the information held by its members into a single summary and passes only that upward — the work of every employee reaches the executives as one department-level result.

Aggregation is attractive because it cuts communication load and keeps the system simple, but it can also degrade control performance. If a swarm of drones or a fleet of automated vehicles were organised this way, what is the right summary for a group to send up? I studied the relationship between information aggregation and optimality for linear systems, and gave a criterion for when the two are compatible.

Asian Journal of Control (2026) · American Control Conference (2022)

How optimally can communication delay be compensated?

Whenever agents exchange information over a network, what each of them receives is already out of date. The natural remedy is to let every agent carry an internal model and predict where its neighbours are now from the delayed measurements it actually holds.

The difficulty is that the prediction itself relies on a model and is produced under the same delay it is meant to cancel. I studied a predictor-based compensation scheme for multi-agent systems and the conditions under which it recovers the behaviour intended by the delay-free design.

Interests

My research aimed at opening up multi-agent systems by bringing abstract algebra together with control theory. The long-term dream behind it has not changed: to realise beautiful motion by exploiting, rather than cancelling, the nonlinearity of dynamics.

  • Nonlinear control theory, including nonholonomic systems
  • Abstract algebra — ring theory, representation theory
  • Differential geometry — manifolds, Lie groups and Lie algebras
  • Data-driven methods and machine learning
